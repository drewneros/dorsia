What caviar is

Caviar is the roe of the sturgeon, lightly salted and packed in tins. Each variety comes from a different sturgeon, and each tastes different: the size of the grain, its colour and its flavour all change from one fish to the next.

Dorsiah's caviar is Persian, from Rasht in the province of Gilan, near the southern shore of the Caspian Sea.

The varieties

  • Beluga

    From the beluga sturgeon. The largest grain, pale to dark grey, soft and creamy with a long, gentle finish.

  • Oscietra

    A medium grain from golden brown to dark, firm, with a rounded, nutty flavour.

  • Sevruga

    From the starry sturgeon. A small, dark grey grain with the most intense, briny taste.

  • Baerii

    From the Siberian sturgeon. A small to medium dark grain, balanced and easy to love, a good first caviar.

General notes on each variety. Dorsiah's own tasting notes for each tin come from the client.

How to serve

Serve it cold, straight from the fridge, and open the tin just before your guests sit down. Use a spoon of mother-of-pearl, horn, bone or glass: silver and steel can leave a metallic taste.

The purest way is on its own, from the spoon or the back of the hand. Blinis and a little crème fraîche are the classic company, with Champagne or ice-cold vodka.

How to store

Keep the tin closed in the coldest part of the fridge, between 0 and 4 °C, and eat it by the date on the lid. Never freeze it.

Once opened, press the lid back on and finish the tin within two days.

General guidance. Check against the supplier's own storage and shelf-life instructions before launch.
